# Future work

Sodalis demonstrates how a conversational companion and a personal desktop
can work together. Its next stage is about turning that technical foundation
into dependable, useful support for everyday life, particularly for older
adults and people with different abilities.

The directions below are opportunities for further research and development,
not commitments or capabilities already available in the prototype.

## Companion reliability and convergence

A valuable companion must understand a question, find relevant information,
and give a clear answer that reflects what is actually known. Improving that
reliability is more important than simply adding more tools. Sodalis should
also make uncertainty understandable and remain responsive when information
is missing or a person changes their mind.

The longer-term ambition is a coherent assistant experience that combines
conversation, personal context, and carefully controlled actions. People
should not need to understand different internal operating modes to benefit
from those capabilities.

## Real integrations and everyday capabilities

Connecting Sodalis to real household systems could extend the simulated-home
experience into practical support: understanding conditions in the home and
helping with appropriate controls. Email, calendar, and reminders offer
another route toward assistance with daily organisation and staying in touch.

Document explanation, message drafting, and grocery-list preparation are
promising examples of useful application support. These opportunities must
preserve user control: access to personal information and consequential
actions require clear permission and appropriate confirmation. Real-home
and personal-account integrations remain future work.

## Memory that remains correct and controllable

Personal context could make Sodalis more helpful over time, allowing it to
remember preferences and avoid asking the same questions repeatedly. The
research challenge is not just remembering more, but remembering accurately,
recognising when circumstances change, and using that information
appropriately.

People need understandable choices about what is remembered, straightforward
ways to correct or forget information, and particular care around sensitive
data. The value of memory should be demonstrated through better everyday
experiences, rather than measured only by retrieval performance.

## Accessible speech, presence, and activation

Further work could make conversation faster, clearer, and more comfortable
across different voices, abilities, and devices. Speech, readable text, and
the graphical interface should complement one another rather than make any
single interaction method essential.

Different presentations, from abstract dots to a restrained human avatar,
offer opportunities to explore comfort and accessibility. No single avatar
has been established as best for older adults. A deliberate physical button
is another possible direction for making activation easier; its form remains
open, and wake-word activation is not the current direction.

## Research validation and deployment readiness

The central question is whether Sodalis helps people accomplish meaningful
tasks with greater confidence and independence. Studies with intended users
should examine usefulness, accessibility, error recovery, and trust, while
recognising that older adults are not one uniform group.

Moving beyond the prototype also requires evidence that the system can be
operated responsibly: protecting personal information, maintaining reliable
service, and making limitations clear. A working demonstration alone does
not establish production readiness or clinical benefit.
